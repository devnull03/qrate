Undescribed photographs are the classic backlog: three thousand scans, a filename each, and no
words to search. Visual search lets you search them anyway. Type `a horse-drawn cart on a dirt
road` and qrate ranks the collection by how well each picture matches, even though no cell says
"cart". Or pick one photo and ask for everything that looks like it.

It all runs on your own computer. No image and no search text leaves it.

## What it is good for

- **Finding the rest of a shoot.** One photo of a parade, and the other twelve rolls of it.
- **Grouping before describing.** Find every image of the old mill, describe one, and copy the
  description to the rest with the Details panel.
- **Finding duplicates and copies.** The same form scanned twice, the print and its negative.
- **Answering reference questions.** "Do you have anything with a streetcar in it?"

It covers anything qrate can preview: photos, the first page of PDFs, video poster frames and
audio cover art.

## Turn it on

1. Press **Ctrl+F** to open search.
2. Turn on the **picture toggle** in the search bar.
3. The first time, qrate offers **Download model**. The model is about 607 MB, downloads once per
   computer, and is checked against a published checksum before it is installed.

qrate then reads each file's thumbnail once to index it, about a tenth of a second per file, in the
background. The index is saved in the project, so it only happens once, and only new or changed
files are read again later.

## Search by description

Type what is *in the picture*, in plain words:

| Works | Doesn't |
| --- | --- |
| `two people shaking hands` | `handshake photo formal` |
| `a group of children outside a school` | `school 1952` |
| `a sailing ship in a harbour` | `accession 1994.12` |

The grid, or the gallery, narrows to the results with the best match first. The **slider** next to
the result count widens or narrows how many you see; drag it left for only the closest matches.

## Find similar items

Right-click a row, or a tile in the gallery, and choose **Find similar items**. qrate ranks every
other file by how much it looks like that one. This is the fastest way to collect the rest of a
photo shoot, the other copies of a form, or the pages that came out of the same album.

## Describe the group in one go

Once the results are the ones you want:

1. Select them, in the grid or the gallery.
2. In the Details panel, type the shared values, such as the subject or the event, once. They go
   into every selected row.
3. Clear the search to bring every row back.

See [Describe your first items](/guides/describe-your-first-items) for multi-row editing.

## Know its limits

- **It ranks, it does not decide.** The model knows nothing about your collection and can be
  confidently wrong. Treat each result as a suggestion to check.
- **It reads one picture per file**: page one of a PDF, one frame of a video.
- **It does not read text in the image**, printed or handwritten. To search words inside PDFs, use
  the book toggle next to the picture toggle.
- **English queries work best**, because the model was trained on English descriptions.
- **It never writes to your data.** It only finds and orders rows.
