**EDTF**, the Extended Date/Time Format, is a way to write the dates archives actually have:
approximate ones, uncertain ones, decades and ranges. It was developed at the Library of Congress
and is now part of ISO 8601. Islandora, many digital asset systems and linked-data tools read it.

Archival dates are rarely exact, and a spreadsheet full of `ca. 1920`, `1920's?` and `[1920]` is
hard for any system to sort or search. EDTF gives each of those one spelling. This guide is a
cheat sheet, then shows how qrate checks a column of EDTF dates and fixes the common slips.

## The EDTF cheat sheet

| You mean | Archivists often write | EDTF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An exact day | 12 March 1939, 3/12/1939 | `1939-03-12` |
| A month | March 1939 | `1939-03` |
| A year | 1939 | `1939` |
| About this date | circa 1920, ca. 1920, c. 1920 | `1920~` |
| Probably this date, not sure | 1920?, [1920?] | `1920?` |
| Both approximate and uncertain | ca. 1920? | `1920%` |
| A decade | 1920s | `192X` |
| A century | 1900s, 20th century | `19XX` |
| A range | 1920–1925 | `1920/1925` |
| A range, open at one end | before 1925 | `../1925` |
| A range, open at one end | 1920 onward | `1920/..` |
| A range, end not known | 1920 to ? | `1920/` |
| One of these years | either 1667 or 1668 | `[1667,1668]` |
| All of these dates | 1960 and December 1961 | `{1960,1961-12}` |

A qualifier at the end covers the whole date: `1939-03~` is "about March 1939", and `1939-03-12?`
is "probably 12 March 1939".

## Why bother

- **Sorting and searching work.** `1920~` sorts with 1920. `ca. 1920` sorts with the c's.
- **The uncertainty survives.** A plain `1920` claims more than you know. `1920~` says exactly as
  much as you know.
- **Systems can read it.** Islandora's date fields, and the linked-data tools around them, expect
  EDTF. Getting it right before an ingest saves a round of rejected rows.

## Check a column of dates in qrate

1. Choose **Data ▸ Column Settings…** and set your date column's type to **Date** under **Data
   types**.
2. Open **Settings ▸ Table ▸ Checks ▸ Date format** and choose **EDTF** (the default).
3. Open the Problems panel with **Ctrl+Shift+M**.

Every value EDTF cannot read is now an error, with the value in the message. Identical mistakes are
grouped, so fifty cells of `ca. 1920` are one line with a count.

## Fix the common slips

For the mistakes that are typing habits rather than questions of fact, qrate offers a rewrite.
Right-click the cell, open **Problems**, and pick the suggestion, such as **Use 1962~**:

| Written | Offered | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `circa 1962`, `ca. 1962`, `c. 1962` | `1962~` | The EDTF approximate marker |
| `1965/04/20`, `1965.04.20` | `1965-04-20` | Hyphens, not slashes or dots |
| `1966-6-4` | `1966-06-04` | Two-digit months and days |

A rewrite is offered only if the result is valid. Anything that needs judgment, such as `1960s`
(the decade, or a guess within it?) or `12/04/1939` (December or April?), gets no suggestion. That
is deliberate: deciding what a date means is the archivist's call, not a menu's. Type the answer,
`196X` or `1939-04-12`, and the error clears.

## When EDTF is too strict

Not every project is headed for an EDTF system. **Settings ▸ Table ▸ Checks ▸ Date format** has
two other modes:

- **ISO 8601 only** accepts `YYYY`, `YYYY-MM`, `YYYY-MM-DD` and a range of two of them. Use it when
  the receiving system cannot read EDTF qualifiers.
- **Lenient** accepts everything EDTF does plus the catalogue habits: `circa 1920`, `ca. 1920`,
  `c. 1920`, `1920s`, `[1920?]` and `[ca. 1920]`. Use it to clean up a legacy sheet in stages,
  catching real errors first and converting spellings later.

A project can override your default, so one EDTF project and one lenient project can sit side by
side.
